Key Rules

The essentials, no filler

The core definitions and rules you actually need to remember — not the full lecture.

Definition

Genetics

Study of genes, genetic variation, and heredity. The genome = total genetic information of an organism.

Central Dogma

Gene Expression

DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation) → protein. The rule linking nucleotide and amino acid sequences is the genetic code.

Rule

DNA vs RNA bases

DNA: Adenine, Thymine, Cytosine, Guanine. RNA: Adenine, Uracil, Cytosine, Guanine — Uracil replaces Thymine.

Rule

Base Pairing

A pairs with T (or U in RNA); G pairs with C. This is complementary base pairing — the basis of heredity.

Rule

Strand Polarity

Each strand runs 5' (free phosphate) → 3' (free hydroxyl). The two DNA strands are antiparallel.

Structure

Phosphodiester Linkage

Phosphate bonds to sugar on both sides by ester linkages → together a phosphodiester linkage. The phosphate carries a negative charge.

Mutation

Spontaneous vs Induced

Spontaneous = occurs naturally. Induced = caused deliberately by a mutagen (chemical or UV radiation).

Mutation Types

6 Sequence Alterations

Substitution, Insertion, Deletion, Inversion, Duplication, Translocation.

Definition

Point / Null Mutation

Point mutation = single base change. Null mutation = completely inactivates the gene.

Rule

Horizontal Gene Transfer

Three mechanisms: Transformation, Transduction, Conjugation. Conjugation is the most common, especially between different species.

Mechanism

Transformation

DNA fragment from a dead, degraded bacterium enters a living recipient; RecA protein drives recombination.

Mechanism

Transduction & Conjugation

Transduction = bacteriophage-mediated DNA transfer (generalized or specialized). Conjugation = direct cell-to-cell transfer via a pilus, usually of a plasmid or transposon.
